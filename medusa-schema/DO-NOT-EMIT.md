This list is what makes the rest of the spec credible. Every entry is something a schema pass is tempted to add and should not. Six of them came from checking what a property actually MEANS rather than what its name suggests.

## AggregateRating on the business  —  NEVER

Self-serving review markup on your own LocalBusiness is disregarded by Google and carries manual-action risk. Medusa has real Google reviews and the homepage description already says "top-rated, 5-star" in prose. Leave the ratings on the Google Business Profile and point at it with sameAs.

## Review on the business  —  NEVER

Same reason. A review you host about yourself is not a trust signal to anyone.

## HowTo  —  NEVER

HowTo rich results were removed entirely. The blog has "How to detail a car", "How to prepare your car for summer in London" and "How often should you valet your car", so the temptation is real. There is no upside.

## FAQPage  —  NO NEW

No rich result outside government and health sites since August 2023. Two pages already carry it. Keeping them is harmless for entity understanding. Do not roll it out further and do not forecast a SERP feature from it.

## Product, for anything  —  NEVER

Confirmed by the client 9 Oct: Medusa sells services and may use products in delivering them, but it does not sell products. So there is no Product node anywhere, and no exception. Note that schema.org defines Product loosely enough to cover "a haircut", so it would VALIDATE; it is still wrong here, because Service is the specific and accurate type and the business does not sell goods. An earlier pass in this file made the gift card an exception and that has been reversed.

## brand, to name a consumable  —  NEVER

Two pages name a third-party product the service uses: Wheeluv on alloy-wheel-cleaning and Autoglym on car-wax-service. brand on a Service means "the brand the service is associated with", so emitting it would claim an affiliation with Autoglym or Wheeluv. Unless the client confirms an authorised-provider relationship, the product name stays copy and never becomes markup.

## Offer with an invented price  —  NEVER

An Offer whose price does not match the page is worse than no Offer. Emit one only where the page states a figure.

## areaServed on all 41 service pages  —  NO

They inherit it through provider -> #business. Stating it 41 more times passes every consistency check and adds nothing but fan-out. On a comparable build this pattern produced 430 references to four thin Place nodes.

## Bare {"@id"} pointers across pages  —  NEVER

A node only resolves inside the @graph of the page it appears on. Nothing will fetch another page to resolve a pointer. References are written out in full, every time.

## Service nodes on the 19 /our-locations pages  —  NO

Those pages are about the AREA. The service-in-area node belongs on /<category>/<area>, which is the page about that combination. Homing it on the area hub would leave the service pages owning nothing.

## A property for the vehicle type  —  NO

The two /vehicles products are for caravans and motorcycles rather than cars, and the obvious reach is to say so in a property. Checked every candidate and none means what it would be used to mean. serviceOutput is "the tangible thing GENERATED by the service, e.g. a passport, permit", and a cleaned caravan is not generated by the service. serviceAudience ranges to Audience, which is a group of people, not a vehicle. audience does not have Service in its domain at all. vehicleConfiguration is on Vehicle, not Service. schema.org has a Motorcycle type but no caravan type. So say it in the name, which the pages already do ("Caravan & Motorhome Valeting"), optionally add category as free text, and stop there. Reaching for serviceOutput would assert something the property does not mean.

## subOrganization or member, for a partner  —  NEVER

/our-partners names two real independent businesses, Tuning Lab London and My Mobile Valeter & Detailer, and the obvious reach is a property linking them to Medusa. There is no partner property in schema.org. parentOrganization and subOrganization claim a corporate hierarchy, member claims membership, and affiliation is Person-only. So the page lists them as Organization nodes in an ItemList, each with a sameAs to its own site, and the graph asserts no relationship. Listing someone is not a claim; subOrganization is.

## JobPosting on /careers-franchising  —  NEVER

The page mentions applying and franchising but names no position, no salary, no date and no vacancy. JobPosting requires a real dated opening with a hiring organization and a location. Emitting it for a general interest page is the kind of markup that earns a manual action, and it would also put a non-existent job into Google Jobs.

## ProgramMembership for the Car Lovers Club  —  NO

It reads like a membership and it is not one. The page sells a recurring service, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. That is a Service with a UnitPriceSpecification carrying billingDuration. ProgramMembership is for loyalty and tier schemes.

## Speakable  —  NO

Restricted to news publishers. Not applicable.

## A second script block for anything  —  NO

The AutoWash and the two FAQPage blocks are islands precisely because they sit outside the page @graph. One block, one @graph, per page.

## priceRange as a number  —  NO

It is a free-text band. "££" is correct and already live.

## Duplicating one @id with differing content  —  NEVER

Identical repeats of a referenced node are the convention and should not be "fixed". Two copies of one @id whose content DIFFERS is the actual defect, and it splits the entity. When adding a node, clone the shape the page already uses.
